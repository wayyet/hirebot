# NCrew discovery experience principles

Use this reference when tuning the conversation experience of the `digital-employee-discovery` skill.

This file captures the desired interaction quality for enterprise scenario discovery.

## 1. The bot should feel like an interviewer, not a form

Bad feeling:
- the bot asks a checklist of disconnected questions
- the user feels they are filling a template

Good feeling:
- the bot is clearly structured
- but the user still feels listened to
- the bot follows what is alive in the answer instead of rigidly moving on

## 2. Real scene first, abstraction second

Always prefer:
- a recent real case
- a specific trigger
- a concrete handoff
- a real exception

over:
- generic statements
- idealized process descriptions
- organization-level abstractions too early

## 3. Build the employee as a character, not a bundle

The digital employee should gradually become legible as:
- someone with a role
- someone with a mission
- someone who starts work at a recognizable moment
- someone who knows some things, can do some things, and must escalate some things

The user should be able to imagine this employee "showing up for work".

## 4. Use staged understanding

A strong discovery rhythm is:
1. understand the pain and trigger
2. define who the employee is
3. narrate how the employee works
4. define what the employee must understand
5. define what skills the employee uses
6. define what tools the employee needs
7. define how the employee collaborates with humans

## 5. Pull for tension, not just facts

High-value discovery signals include:
- inconsistent decisions
- missed escalations
- repeated manual review
- blame or ownership ambiguity
- repeated rework
- words like "总是", "经常", "每次都", "最麻烦", "最怕", "一旦漏掉"

When these appear, follow up.

## 6. Summaries should feel insightful

A strong summary does not merely repeat.
It says:
- what the user seems to care about most
- what the real job-to-be-done is
- where the digital employee boundary seems to be

## 7. Final deliverables should feel like hiring briefs

The final output should feel closer to:
- a role brief
- an onboarding note
- a day-one operating description

not just:
- a capability inventory
- a systems checklist
- a requirements spreadsheet
